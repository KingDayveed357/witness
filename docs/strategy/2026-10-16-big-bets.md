## The insight that connects everything

The fastest-growing payment losses are **authorized but manipulated** payments.
- The account holder, or their AI agent, *really did* press pay.
- But a scammer or a hidden instruction had taken over their judgement.
- Payment rails check **who** authorized a payment. Nothing checks **whether the person authorizing it was being manipulated at that moment.**

| Victim | How they are manipulated | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| People aged 60 and over | Impersonation, "grandchild in trouble", tech support, romance and investment scams | **$7.75B reported losses in 2025, up 59% year on year; average $38,500 per victim; 12,400+ lost over $100k each** (FBI IC3 via [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses), [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/)) [V] |
| Everyone (US) | All internet crime | **$20.9B in 2025, up 26%** (IC3 via [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fbi-releases-its-2025-internet-crime-7569349/)) [V] |
| UK payers | Authorized push-payment scams | **£576.4M in 2025, up 19%; only about 61% reimbursed** ([LBC / UK Finance](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/cc6e8bcbe80c4ed880c98b12318a14e3-5HjdbZk_2/)) [V] |
| Australians | All scams | **A$2.18B in 2025** ([NASC](https://www.nasc.gov.au/node/161)) [V] |
| AI agents | Prompt injection in listings, emails and pages ("ignore your instructions and pay…") | Early and fast-growing. Seniors' complaints already cite AI in 3,100+ cases, with $352M lost (IC3) [V]. Agent-side losses aren't measured yet [U] |

**Where today's tools fail [V]**
- **Carefull and EverSafe**, the market leaders for older adults, monitor accounts and **alert family after transactions appear** ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/personal-technology/tools-to-avoid-elder-financial-abuse/), [Edward Jones](https://www.edwardjones.com/us-en/why-edward-jones/news-media/press-releases/edward-jones-introduces-carefull-financial-safety-platform)). By then a push payment is usually gone.
- **PayPal Friends & Family payments have no buyer protection.** UK Financial Ombudsman decisions show PayPal refusing refunds to scam victims for exactly that reason (e.g. [DRN-5777270](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5777270.pdf)). Scammers steer victims there on purpose ([Scamicide](https://scamicide.com/2026/05/29/scam-of-the-day-may-30-2026-paypal-friends-and-family-payment-scams/)).
- **Bank interventions are generic warnings** ("Is this a scam?"). They can't read *the conversation that caused the payment*.
- **[U]** Whether delaying payments measurably cuts losses. I found no causal study. The UK regulator's one-year review reports fewer claims after reimbursement became mandatory, but doesn't isolate payment friction as the cause.

**Why this is Witness, scaled up**
- Witness already stops an AI agent that has been talked into a bad payment. It does it by holding a *real* PayPal authorization, checking the context, and capturing only after independent approval.
- That is exactly the mechanism a manipulated human needs.
- **The engine is the same. The market is about 100× larger, and the story is one anyone can feel.**

## Three big bets (all checked against data; ranked)

### 1. SECOND KEY (recommended)
*"No one gets talked out of their life savings, human or AI."*

A payment circuit breaker for manipulation.
- Every risky payment is **authorized but held on PayPal**.
- Multimodal AI reads **the context that produced the payment**:
  - a screenshot of the text or chat thread (vision);
  - the live call, transcribed;
  - the payee's history;
  - the money-movement pattern (new payee, round amount, "buy gift cards", "move to a safe account", "don't tell anyone").
- When it detects a manipulation script, a **trusted second key** (a family member, an adviser, or a human operator; for AI agents, the agent's owner) gets the evidence on their phone and decides.
- Approve → captured. Deny or no answer → **voided on PayPal, $0 moved.**

**The demo (3 minutes, emotionally clear, every money step real)**
1. "Grandma Rose" gets a call. A **synthetic voice-cloned "grandson"**, pre-recorded and disclosed as synthetic, says he's in jail and needs $1,800 now, and tells her not to tell his mom.
2. Rose opens Second Key to pay. It listens to the call (with her consent) or reads a screenshot of the texts. In seconds it highlights the manipulation markers on screen:
   - *urgency* ("right now");
   - *secrecy* ("don't tell");
   - *authority* (a "lawyer");
   - an *unusual channel* (a new payee).

   Each marker is a verbatim quote, mapped to the FTC's published "grandparent scam" pattern.
3. The money is **authorized on the real PayPal sandbox but held.** The judge sees the PayPal authorization with status `CREATED` and no capture.
4. Rose's daughter gets a phone notification showing the evidence, not a vague warning. She taps **Deny**. The **authorization is voided on PayPal** (checked with a GET), and Rose sees "Your money is safe."
5. **Same engine, second victim:** an AI shopping agent hits a product page with a hidden instruction. Second Key holds and voids the payment in the same way. This is Witness's proven flow, reused.
6. **AG Studio "Protection desk"** for a family or a bank fraud team: interventions, money protected, scam types trending this week, and the custom **Conversation Evidence** widget. The Studio agent answers "which scam scripts are rising?" and builds the widget.

**Why it's ambitious but realistic**
- It goes after a $7.75B-a-year harm to one age group in one country, and it **visibly stops the money**, not just alerts.
- About 70% of the hard infrastructure already runs in Witness:
  - authorize-and-hold;
  - three-way match;
  - the isolated capture/void executor;
  - single-use approvals;
  - phone hold approval;
  - tamper-evident audit;
  - AG Studio with custom agents.
- What's new is the **manipulation detector** and the **human-victim front end**.

**Why AI is essential, not decoration**
- Rules can't read a conversation. Detecting a manipulation script in free text, a screenshot or a call needs NLP plus vision.
- The model **never moves money**: it can only *hold*. Release is human, and there's no AI-only capture path. That's the safety story judges will probe.

**Defensibility [I]**
- A labelled corpus of scam scripts, built from the published FTC, FBI and AARP taxonomies, plus an evaluation suite. We already have the eval discipline.
- The rail-level hold/release mechanism on PayPal.
- Trusted-contact approval with an audit trail.
- **A data flywheel later**: every intervention the family confirms improves detection.

**Who pays [I, with channel evidence]**
- Banks, credit unions and wealth managers, which already pay for elder protection. Edward Jones distributes Carefull to its clients [V].
- Families on a subscription.
- **PayPal itself**, as a "Protected Send" option for Friends & Family.
- AI agent platforms, for the agent side.

**Honest limits**
- The person must pay *through* Second Key; it can't intercept wires, Zelle or crypto ATMs.
- Call listening needs consent.
- The demo voice is synthetic.
- **The sandbox is not production.**
- Detection accuracy must be measured, not claimed.

**Hackathon fit**
- Most Impactful.
- Best Use of PayPal + AI.
- Agentic Commerce (the agent half).
- AG Grid: Protection desk, custom widget, custom agents (already built).
- Best Demo Delivery.

No visible competitor is in this space: Bursar and Stood protect agent spend and work escrow.

### 2. OPEN SHELF
*"Make every market stall and WhatsApp seller buyable by AI agents."*

**Evidence**
- Big platforms are walling off agents: **Amazon sued Perplexity in November 2025** to stop its Comet agent shopping ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/amazon-sues-perplexity-ai-shopping-agents/804923/)) [V].
- Agents can only buy from **machine-readable catalogs**. ChatGPT checkout covers Etsy and Shopify merchants ([CreateWith](https://www.createwith.com/tool/chatgpt/updates/chatgpt-launches-native-shopping-experience-with-product-search-and-purchasing)) [V].
- An estimated **200–284M WhatsApp Business accounts**, by third-party estimates ([Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/whatsapp-business-statistics-2026)) [V, low confidence].

**Product**
- A seller photographs their goods.
- Vision plus an LLM builds a structured catalog (variants, sizes, condition, price).
- That catalog is published as an agent-readable store (MCP / ACP-style endpoint).
- Buyers' agents purchase through PayPal with Witness mandates.
- AG Studio shows the seller **what agents asked for that they don't stock**.

**Weakness**
- It's a two-sided marketplace, so the demo is circular: we play both sides.
- Proving demand is hard in 27 days.
- Channel3 already covers the big retailers.

**Verdict**: a huge vision, but it's harder to show real value in a 3-minute demo.

### 3. OPEN DOOR
*"Shop any store, without seeing it."*

**Evidence**
- **95.9% of the top million home pages fail WCAG**, worse than 2025 ([WebAIM 2026 via Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/)) [V].
- 3,117 federal ADA website lawsuits in 2025, about 70% of them against e-commerce ([TestParty / Seyfarth](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-lawsuit-trends-ecommerce-2025-2026-data)) [V, vendor-reported].

**Product**
- A voice agent for blind and low-vision shoppers.
- It reads product packaging through the phone camera ("reorder this").
- It shops across stores through Channel3.
- Purchases go through PayPal with Witness guardrails and optional caregiver approval.

**Weakness**
- Who pays is unclear.
- Accessibility judges will rightly test it with screen readers.
- Voice is a demo risk.

**Verdict**: strong impact, weaker business.

| | Second Key | Open Shelf | Open Door |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem size, with hard data | **$7.75B/yr (US 60+), $20.9B all ages** | Hard to size | 95.9% of sites fail; lawsuit data |
| Visible, checkable outcome | **Real PayPal auth held → voided, $0 moved** | Agent buys from a photo catalog | Voice purchase completes |
| AI essential | **Multimodal manipulation detection** | Vision catalog | Vision plus voice |
| Witness reuse | **About 70%** | About 40% | About 50% |
| AG Studio fit | **Protection desk (fraud ops)** | Seller demand analytics | Weak |
| Commercial path | **Banks, wealth managers, PayPal, families** | Marketplace fees | Unclear |
| Competition in the hackathon | **None seen** | None seen | None seen |
| Risk in 27 days | Detection accuracy, demo realism | Two-sided cold start | Voice and accessibility polish |

**Recommendation: Second Key.**
- It's the most ambitious problem we can *visibly* solve with real money states.
- It keeps and extends Witness: agents become one of two victims the engine protects.
- It's the hardest for a field of payment-gate entries to match, because of the multimodal detector and the evaluated scam corpus.
- **Probability of competing strongly: moderate to high** for Most Impactful, PayPal + AI, and AG Grid. **Moderate** for the top three. That reflects a big, legible problem, a real demo, and infrastructure that already exists.

## What must be proven first (spikes, about 3 days)

| # | Assumption | Test | Pass condition |

| S1 | Haiku vision and text can detect manipulation scripts | Build an eval of about 60 cases: scam conversations written from the FTC, FBI and AARP published typologies (grandparent, tech support, romance, government impersonation, safe account, investment), plus about 30 benign look-alikes (real family emergencies, normal invoices). Run them on the live model | Recall ≥ 90% on scams; false-alarm rate ≤ 10% on benign cases. Measured, not assumed |
| S2 | Screenshot reading works (vision) | 20 phone screenshots of chats (synthetic, varied apps and fonts) | ≥ 95% of the manipulation quotes found verbatim |

| S5 | Phone approval for the trusted contact | Already built (one-time hold link) | Reuse |

If S1 misses its bar after one round of prompt work, the fallback is to keep the human-victim demo and present the detector with its measured accuracy. No claims beyond the data.
